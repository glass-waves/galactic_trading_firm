Why this round. The live book (v18) takes about 0.4 short trades per session and nothing else. The goal was to add volume while keeping the profit factor healthy. Four ideas from the proposal list were tested in parallel on the IEX bar cache, the feed the trader actually consumes: a self-calibrating VPIN floor, tiered sizing below the floor, longs gated on an up-regime, and relative-strength longs. One unplanned follow-up came out of the regime study and turned out to be the most interesting result of the night. Bar to pass: pooled PF ≥ 1.3 on IEX, at least 4 of 5 years positive, more trades than the baseline's 407; for long-side studies the long subset itself must be positive with PF ≥ 1.2 and no year worse than −300. Reading the tables: every row is a full five-year replay with honest costs, 36 % research sizing and next-bar fills; per-year cells show P&L with trade count and PF; the highlighted row is the study's best cell. Δn and ΔP&L are against the baseline. One caveat that applies to every combined-book row: the replay runs tickers independently, so the live three-position cap is not simulated; with four names the cap binds rarely, but the long-plus-short totals are an upper bound.

What the grid says

  • The raw 0.217 floor sits at roughly the 82nd to 85th percentile of IEX VPIN readings, so every grid cell with P ≤ 0.7 is a looser filter: 521 to 689 trades at PF 1.24 to 1.34 instead of 407 at 1.48.
  • The window length W does not matter: at a given P the three windows agree within five trades and 0.03 of PF. The percentile is a property of the level, not the memory.
  • The like-for-like cell (P 0.8, 440 trades) is statistically the same strategy as the raw floor: PF 1.44 vs 1.48, P&L +2,216 vs +2,154, a smaller drawdown. The extra trades between P 0.8 and P 0.5 are worth about $60 in total: pure churn.
  • The steadier-trade-rate hypothesis is false. The coefficient of variation of yearly trade counts rises from 0.07 (raw floor) to 0.10 at P 0.8 and 0.18 at P 0.4. VPIN is not the binding condition; the thrust conditions fire about 40 % more often in 2022 and loosening VPIN amplifies that.
  • 2023 is negative in every cell and 2024 is flat in every cell. This knob does not touch the two weak years.
  • Live-parity prerequisite: the percentile history lives inside the engine process and the trader restarts every morning. The live warm-up seeds candle windows but does not tick the engine, so on a live day the percentile would not exist until about 12:45 ET, after the entry cutoff. Adopting it needs a warm-up replay through the engine (small code change) before it can go live.
Verdict. P 0.8 with W 1950 nominally passes (PF 1.44, 4 of 5 years, 440 trades) but the fourth positive year is 2024 at +$33 and the cell is a restatement of the fixed floor, not an improvement. Worth adopting only as feed-portable plumbing for the 0.217 floor, expecting parity, and only after the engine warm-up change. It is not a volume lever.

What the sub-floor trades are

Tier-2 trades (VPIN between L and 0.217 at entry) are 30 to 47 % of each cell's trades and run at PF 1.18 to 1.29 at reduced size, about the same as at full size in the earlier lower-floor sweeps. They split into two populations. About 30 % are front-runners: VPIN crosses L and then 0.217 a median three to four minutes later, so the trade is one v18 would have taken anyway, entered earlier and smaller (PF 2.6 to 3.5, +$0.85 per share vs +$0.29 for the v18 trade it replaces, but at a third to two-thirds of the size). About 70 % are genuinely new: VPIN never reaches 0.217 during the hold; PF 0.58 to 0.69, −$0.28 per share, 92 % from the 5m-thrust window, negative in four of five years in every cell. Lowering L does not improve quality, it doubles both populations. Every displaced v18 trade (64 to 95 per cell, worth +423 to +528) was displaced because the same ticker was already open in a tier-2 position; the three-position cap displaced nothing. The +223 of the cap-45 % follow-up comes entirely from upsizing 220 of v18's own trades with VPIN ≥ 0.26, which is leverage on v18, not tiering.

What the grid says

  • Every cell keeps PF ≥ 1.36 and adds 80 to 186 trades, and every cell loses money against the baseline (−122 to −332). Volume up, P&L down.
  • 2023 and 2024 stay negative in every cell; the sub-floor trades do not help the weak years.
  • The sub-floor profit lives entirely in the front-runners, trades where VPIN is about to cross the floor. That is a lead for a separate study: a VPIN-slope condition, or upgrading a sub-floor entry to full size when VPIN crosses 0.217 while the position is open.
  • The 1.25× top tier for VPIN ≥ 0.26 is worth testing on v18 alone at a 45 % cap: +345 on those 220 trades. It is a sizing question, not a volume one.
Verdict. Best cell L 0.12 · M 0.67 (+2,032, 593 trades, PF 1.36) passes PF and trade count but has only 3 positive years and −122 against v18. Do not promote. Keep the 0.217 floor at full size.

What the grid says

  • On IEX the filtered long book is not the loser it was on SIP: +775 over five years. But that is PF 1.06 and +$0.62 per trade against +$5.28 for the shorts, with a −1,387 drawdown. Noise-level edge.
  • Regime gating inverts the premise. Splitting the ungated long trades by regime flag shows that the SMA-50, 20-day-return and low-vol gates keep the losing half (PF 0.93 to 0.96) and discard the profitable half (+1,139 to +1,238, PF 1.13 to 1.32). The longs earn on bear-market bounces (March, July and October 2022), not on drift. This is what prompted Study C2 below.
  • 2025 is a hole no regime fixes: long −412 to −753, concentrated in May to August 2025 while SPY sat above its averages.
  • The v17 filters matter on the long side too. Dropping VPIN adds 585 trades worth about −1,050; widening SPY to ±0.3 % adds 97 worth about −400; dropping the candle window is neutral because the strong-core window re-takes the setups.
  • Strong core is churn on the long side (755 to 984 trades, PF about 1.03). Long P&L comes from timeout and session-close exits, while score exits (36 trades, −1,798) and hard stops (10 trades, −940) destroy it. NVDA carries the long book (+1,664); AMZN (−804) and MSFT (−591) drag it.
  • The short subset was identical to v18 in every cell (407 trades, P&L within ±4), so the long windows never displaced a short.
Verdict. No cell passes. The best regime, above SMA 20, fails combined PF (1.18), positive years (3), long PF (1.05) and the −300 year floor (2025 −753). It passes only on trade count. Up-regime gating is the wrong idea for these names.

Removing the breakeven stop, raising its trigger to 1 %, a 1.5 % or 4 % hard stop, cutting losers at 25 minutes, holding winners to the close, or a 0.4 % trailing stop all land between −50 and −1,150. The five hard-stopped longs never came back above entry and the score-exited ones did so 18 % of the time, so a looser stop would have lost more. The exit stack is not the lever for the long book; the entries are.
Verdict. Below SMA 50 passes every number in the bar but was found after the fact and leans on 2022. Not promotable tonight. It is the one candidate from this round worth a proper validation: leave-one-year-out over the regime family (SMA 50, SMA 100, 20-day return, drawdown from the 52-week high), per-ticker inclusion, and the regime definition's neighbours (SMA 100, drawdown from the 52-week high). If it survives, it is the v19 candidate: the v18 shorts plus bear-bounce longs.

What the grid says

  • Relative strength is the best single long filter tried on IEX: rel ≥ 0.3 % lifts the long subset from +775 (PF 1.06, worst year −652) to +1,303 (PF 1.11, worst year −356) and gives five positive combined years. But PF 1.11 on the long side is still thin.
  • Tighter is monotonically worse: the edge lives in the 0.3 to 0.5 % band, and names up 1.5 % against SPY mean-revert (long subset −298).
  • VPIN carries most of the long edge: removing it turns the long subset into −1,573 on 2,760 trades.
  • The mirrored short spread leg is a loser in all five years (−1,755, PF 0.87) and, worse, it pre-empts the v18 short windows, cutting them from 407 to 143 trades. A short spread window must never sit at a priority above the v18 windows.
  • The candle-reversal window is the only long window with PF above 1.2, on 30 to 82 trades over five years.
  • Same exit pattern as Study C: long profit comes from timeouts and session closes; the 3 to 4 % of trades that hit score exits or hard stops give back about two-thirds of it. Average hold 54 minutes, median 41.
  • 2025 is negative for every long variant.
Verdict. Best cell rel ≥ 0.3 % on the three windows (+3,458 combined, 1,484 trades, PF 1.21, 5 of 5 years) fails the bar on combined PF (1.21), long PF (1.11) and the −300 year floor (−356). Not promotable. Relative strength is worth keeping as an ingredient (it is the best long filter found) but not as the long book's entry logic.

Conclusion and recommendation

The short-side knobs are exhausted at the VPIN margin. The percentile floor restates the fixed floor (parity at the 80th percentile, churn below it) and tiered sizing adds trades that lose money in aggregate. The sub-floor profit sits entirely in trades where VPIN is about to cross the floor, which is a lead for a VPIN-slope study, not a reason to lower the floor. The v18 short book stays as it is.

The long side has one live lead. Up-regime gating fails, relative strength is a good filter but not an entry, and both fail the long-PF bar. The inverse gate, longs only after a month or more of SPY selling, is the only configuration tonight to clear every number: combined PF 1.40 with 4 of 5 years positive, and a long subset of +1,239 on 331 trades at PF 1.32. It was found after seeing the data and it leans on 2022, so it goes to validation, not promotion.

Recommended next steps, in order.

  1. Validate the bear-bounce long on the IEX cache: leave-one-year-out across the regime family (SMA 50, SMA 100, 20-day return, drawdown from the 52-week high); per-ticker inclusion. Accept if the long subset stays PF ≥ 1.2 with 2022 held out and no year worse than −300. If it passes, it becomes v19: v18 shorts plus bear-bounce longs, with the same three-position cap.
  2. Do not spend the next sweep on long exits. The follow-up counterfactual on the bear-bounce entries shows the configured stack is a local optimum: every looser, tighter, trailing or breakeven variant tested does worse, and the stopped-out longs never recovered. The agents' observation that score exits and hard stops carry the long losses is where the losses land, not a fixable exit rule.
  3. VPIN-slope condition as a short-side volume study: enter when VPIN is rising through the band just under the floor, the population that carried Study B's only profit.
  4. Sizing, separately from volume: 1.25× on VPIN ≥ 0.26 at a 45 % cap is worth +345 on v18's own trades. Test it on v18 alone; it is a risk decision, not a volume one.
  5. Percentile VPIN as plumbing only, after an engine warm-up replay exists, to make the floor feed-independent. Expect parity.

What did not change. 2023 is negative and 2024 is flat in every short-side cell of the night. No knob in this round touched that, which says the weakness in those years is in the trigger, not the filters.
