## 3. Live vs replay: MISMATCH

| ticker | live entry | replay entry (signal → fill) | live exit | replay exit | match |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NVDA | 09:35 | 09:35 → 09:36 | max_hold 10:16 | max_hold 10:17, −1.47 | ✅ |
| AMZN | 09:40 | 09:35 → 09:36 | max_hold 10:25 | breakeven 10:27, −1.26 | ❌ live 4–5 bars late |
| MSFT | 09:35 | 09:49 → 09:50 | max_hold 10:19 | max_hold 10:31, −7.99 | ❌ live 14 bars early |

The binding difference is the VPIN ≥ 0.217 filter:

- **AMZN.** Replay VPIN was 0.21 at 09:30 and 0.23 at 09:34–35, so it opened on the 09:35 bar. Live near-miss rows show VPIN at 0.08 (09:30) and 0.07 (09:35), both blocked, and live entered at 09:40.
- **MSFT.** Replay VPIN stayed at 0.04–0.18 on every bar from 09:30 to 09:48 and first reached 0.33 at 09:49. Live passed the VPIN filter at 09:35.
- **Root cause.** I confirmed this in code, and it upgrades the 13:30 memo's hypothesis. The live stream subscribes to **IEX** bars (`alpaca_feed.rs:120`), but startup warmup pages **SIP** bars (`alpaca_feed.rs:199–209`). The replay cache is SIP throughout. VPIN sizes its buckets as a fraction of average volume and classifies by bar volume. Early in the session, live windows mix SIP bars from the day before with today's IEX bars, which carry only ~2–5 % of SIP volume. So live VPIN is a different quantity from the one the 0.217 threshold was fitted on, and the gap is largest during 09:30–10:00, which is exactly when this book trades.
- **Why it matters.** The VPIN floor is half of what took v16 from PF 1.12 to v17 at PF 1.64. Trade counts still agree (3 vs 3 today, 1 vs 1 on 09-23, 0 vs 0 from 09-15 to 09-22), so this is not a gross break. But live is not quite trading the backtested strategy.
